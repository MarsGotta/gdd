# Proposing parts before the brief

When the person asks for parts or you see several things they could use separately, propose the parts before you brief any. Give each part one line with what they get and roughly how long it takes. Use earlier logs for the estimates or say you're guessing. The first part must give them something they can see or try. Ask one question they can answer with a yes (`--of decision`). Wait for that yes before briefing any part.

    <What they can see or try first> (<rough time>)
    <What they get next> (<rough time>, one line per remaining part)
    <One question asking for their yes to these parts>

    First, you can play an A to tune your instrument (about 15 minutes).
    Then, you can set a beat to practise to (about 25 minutes).
    Those times are my guesses. Shall I deliver these as two changes in that order?

Without a clear yes, brief the whole request as one change. That includes a no, “you decide” or no answer. Don't ask about parts again. Never split a request on your own or split a change already under way.

With their yes, each part gets its own brief and approval and goes through its gates. Splitting doesn't lower the risk. Each part takes the color of what it touches, leaves the product working and can be undone on its own. Put anything that delivers nothing yet behind a switch. Turning it on is a separate change at the risk level of what it turns on.
