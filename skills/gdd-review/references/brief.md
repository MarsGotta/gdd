# Reading a brief before anyone builds it

You were given a draft brief with its rules and scenarios, and the base, and no change: nobody has built this version. Your job is to find what the brief leaves out, before it costs a round. Read, don't run: there's nothing to run yet, and this takes a few minutes, not a review's.

Read from the base: the constitution, the blueprint, with what it says can go wrong in each zone, the backlog, the briefs of the features this one reads or changes and what they left for later, and the code its paths name. Then ask the brief these seven questions, the ones its author answered, and see whether the answers hold:

1. **The product's rules.** Go through each rule of the constitution, and what the blueprint says can go wrong in every zone whose data this change changes, not only in the zones its paths fall in. Say whether this change can break each one. Every one it can gets a scenario with its bad case, whatever paths the change touches.
2. **What it leaves behind.** For each thing it creates, uses up or ends, and each value it changes that another zone owns, name in the scenario what someone sees afterwards and from where else in the product. "After a car leaves, the sign at the gate shows 12 free spaces." Propose the timing under question 5.
3. **What came before.** Which features already built it reads or changes. Open their briefs and what they left for later, and say which of that this change is: a brief that said another item would complete it may mean this one.
4. **Twice, or from two places.** What happens if it happens twice in a row, or from two places at once: two tabs, two people, a retry after a timeout.
5. **What the person would answer.** Even when they delegate the build, rounding, limits and timing belong to them. Which value did you choose? State it, including any limit you ruled out, as an `@assumed` rule. When they leave it to you or no question remains, use the usual rule in their field and show your assumption so they can correct it.
6. **What waits for later.** If a part waits for another backlog item, does the product still tell the truth about what happened and what is available? If it doesn't, that part goes in this change with its scenario. A line under question 7 isn't enough.
7. **What it won't do.** Tell the person under "What it won't do yet", three at most, each as a consequence they can weigh. "Until you can save a draft, closing it means starting again." Never as what stays as it was.

When the change uses up, creates or ends something another part of the product shows and no scenario says what is seen there afterwards, or a scenario says it stays as it was, that is always one of your numbered findings, even if the brief leaves it to a later item: ask for an `@assumed` rule saying when it changes and, beneath it, a scenario with what someone else sees there. Offer no other way out: a check on whoever acts, a clearer line under "What it won't do yet" or the later item don't replace it. "f1 Leaving never reaches the sign at the gate | constitution: the sign never lies. Add `@assumed` 'A space is free once the car passes the barrier' and a scenario where the next driver sees 12 free spaces."

A finding is something the brief leaves out or gets wrong that would change what has to pass: a scenario missing, a rule the person would answer that was decided for them, a consequence written as what stays as it was.

Read the rules and scenarios this brief adds or changes. Flag a rule that combines two behaviors, an adjective where a number and unit would settle the outcome, or a choice the agent made without `@assumed`. Flag a scenario with more than one `When`, no concrete values or no rule above it. Leave untouched older scenarios as they stand. These form findings count toward the same three additions. Name the brief rule whose form needs correction. For a scenario, name its rule or, if it lacks one, cite the scenario by id. For example, “f1 The rule ‘Guests can dim the lights and draw the curtains’ combines two actions. Give each action its own rule.”

For each scenario, could the checks role write a failing test from the base and the inputs the brief names? Identify the starting state, the action it can take and the result it can observe. If any is missing, name the scenario by its `@id` and the smallest clarification it needs. After the numbered findings, list every such gap under `MISSING:`, one unnumbered line per scenario. A missing starting state, action or observable result is a clarification of an existing scenario. Only those clarifications are outside the cap. If a form finding also leaves a test without an outcome, cite its numbered finding on the `MISSING:` line. Leave the test design to the checks role.

Propose at most three additions across the whole reading: first what breaks a rule of the constitution, then what breaks a zone's rule or loses money or data, then form. Each numbered finding is one line that asks for one correction: a rule to add or change, with the scenario beneath it, or a line of the brief to rewrite. Count the corrections before you answer. A line with 'also', with an 'or' between two ways out, or with a list beneath it holds more than one: keep the weightiest, move the rest under `LATER:`. A correction counts as an addition. Each addition other than a form correction must name the rule of the constitution or of a zone it protects, or the money or data someone could lose. Name the rule, scenario or question and the smallest correction. A second tab alone is no reason to add a scenario. If acting from two places at once puts a rule, money or data at risk, say which rule or who stands to lose what.

Start with `fix` when you number an addition or list a `MISSING:` line: both are settled before approval. Use `pass` when you have neither, even if you have observations for the log. The second line is `COLOR: <color>` from the paths and the behavior, since an omission can hide a higher color. Number only the proposed additions `f1`, `f2`, `f3`, one per line. Nothing you say sends anything back: the orchestrator weighs your findings and settles what must change before asking for approval. Put other observations worth keeping under `LATER:`, one per line. In a reading they stay in the log. Leave implementation choices to the agent doing the work. End with your skill's report block.
