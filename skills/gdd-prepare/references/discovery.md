# Shaping the idea together

For when the person isn't sure yet what they want to make, or says so. It is the first part of preparing, where they decide what the product is for. It has no gate and no approval: it ends when you can write the five lines of "The product" in the constitution.

An idea in one line from someone who knows what they want is not this, nor are stories or tickets already written. Fill the five lines from them, as `new-project.md` says, ask only for what none of them says, such as the biggest doubt, and propose.

It is a conversation, and it should feel like one. Its questions are talk and not decisions of the method: they need no `gdd ask`, and they don't have to be answerable with a yes. The first things the method records are what the gates should read, asked with the first proposal, and then the approval of the blueprint.

- **Let them tell it their way first.** Invite them to pour it all out, in any order, and listen for what they care about most.
- **Then one question at a time,** and only those whose answer would change what gets made. While you don't yet know what they want it for, ask openly, with no answers attached. Once you do, offer two or three ways it could go and say which one you'd take and why.
- **Say back what you understood,** in their words, every few turns. It shows them you followed, and it lets them correct you early.
- **Go at their pace.** Someone who answers in a line wants to move on, and someone who thinks out loud wants room. Either can stop you at any moment and tell you to go ahead with what you have.
- **Recommend, and say what you'd leave for later.** A first version that does one thing well is worth more than a plan for everything.
- **Something to look at, when words aren't enough.** If they can't tell what they want until they see it, offer a throwaway draft: a sketch, a mock screen, a sample of the text, whatever the recipe of their area in `../gdd-recipes/` says a draft is there. Make it in `.gdd/drafts/`, which git leaves out, so it is never committed, sealed or merged, and no builder ever sees it. Say what it is when you show it: a draft to react to, not the product, and it will be thrown away. Their reaction is what you keep, in the five lines; the first real change is briefed as if nothing had been built.

Stop when what is left to ask wouldn't change the five lines: who it is for, what happens to them today, what the first version leaves out, how you'll both know it serves, and the biggest doubt. Write them in the constitution as its template says. Tell them back in two or three sentences, saying what you assumed, and ask whether that is it. "Not known yet" is still an answer, and it stays in sight.

Keep what you learned about how they like to work in their profile, as the `gdd` skill's reference on accompanying the person says. Then go on with preparing: look things up as `research.md` says, and from here on propose first.
