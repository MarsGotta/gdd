The five moments

When each thing happens, from day one until something slips through.

Before you start, stop 5 of 5

MomentWhenWhat happens
PrepareAt the start, and when the blueprint falls shortThe blueprint is defined: constitution, delicate zones, gates and checks. Then the first gates get built, and you try to beat them, and the rest come when a change needs them.
BriefOn every change, before it startsThe rules the change touches, a few scenarios and the initial risk are written down.
ExecuteOn every changeThe agent gets through the gates one by one. If it gets stuck, it stops and asks. If the risk goes up, it goes back to Brief.
CloseWith the change already in useYou look at whether it helped, against what was written when it was briefed. The verdict belongs to a person: yes, not yet or no.
HardenWhen something slips through or a gate stops adding anythingWhat slipped through becomes a check or a rule. What isn't needed is relaxed or retired. The blueprint changes, so you go back to Prepare.

A change, in seven steps

  1. You decide what has to pass.
  2. An agent writes the cases, which must fail for the right reason.
  3. Another agent gets through the gate without touching the checks.
  4. The gate closes out of its reach.
  5. A person approves where the blueprint asks for it.
  6. You look at whether it helped.
  7. If something slips through, you harden.

Your blueprint decides how much each step weighs.

When a change is closed

There is no deadline. While nobody looks, the change waits in plain sight: it doesn't close by itself, it isn't canceled and it doesn't hold up what comes next. A change that promises nothing to whoever uses it, like a typo, closes when its time passes without doing harm.

When the blueprint falls short

What the blueprint doesn't cover never comes out green. An agent that won't do the work analyzes it, writes down why and, if it isn't sure, asks. Relaxing a check is never decided inside a change.
