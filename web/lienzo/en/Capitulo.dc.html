The idea

Why the agent's "done" isn't enough, and what to do about it.

Before you start, stop 1 of 5About 4 minutes

You ask the agent for something, it works for a good while and at the end it tells you "done." You try it, and it isn't. You ask more carefully, you write it longer specifications, and it tells you "done" again with the same confidence. You end up reviewing everything yourself, which was exactly what you wanted to avoid.

I spent a long time like that, until I stopped trying to convince it and started putting gates on it.

What a gate is

A gate is what the agent has to prove before moving on. You decide it before it starts: that a customer gets charged only once, that the total never drops below zero. Its word opens nothing.

Check
What opens the gate. Something you already use and the agent can't touch: a test, a linter, another person's review. You get through the gate if they all pass.
Context
What the agent needs to know to get through it. It helps it get through, but it proves nothing.
Risk
What can go wrong with the change. It decides how many checks are needed and whether you get called.
Harden
When something slips through, adding the check that would have stopped it, and relaxing or retiring the one that no longer catches anything.

Think of it as an escape room

Each door has its puzzle, the clues are the context and the key only shows up once the puzzle is solved. The locks change with the difficulty: some are watched by a machine, others by a person.

You don't play: you design the game. The locks are your checks, the clues are the context and the players are your agents. If someone cheats or a room turns out too easy, you reinforce that gate.

If the change is a typo, you barely notice. If it touches payment, you ask for a lot more, and it may call you. The table is what I would do, and what the template brings: what you approve in each color is up to you, or your team.

What you design

We don't develop code, or even tests. We write what must be true and design the gates our agents go through.

Specifications don't disappear: they become executable. They're written as rules and scenarios, which a person can read and a machine can check. An agent can draft them; you review them and correct them.

Rule: SUMMER10 takes 10% off the cart
@id:discounts-1
Scenario: a 10% discount code
  Given a €50.00 cart
  When I apply the code SUMMER10
  Then the total is €45.00

It's the shift OpenAI describes when it talks about the agent's environment: "Humans steer. Agents execute." OpenAI, 2026

Before you cross: the agent says "done," and the tests it ran itself pass. Does the gate open?

It ran them itself, so it's still its word. What opens the gate is a check it can't change, run out of its reach: CI, or you in a clean copy.

That's it. The gate is opened by a check out of its reach, not by what it tells you.
