How it is measured

A method that demands facts from agents can't ask you for an act of faith.

Close, stop 1 of 1

Four things to prove

WhatThe questionHow it's measured
LoadDoes it take work off you?Minutes of work per change, the ones it spends with all its gates already green, the agent's questions and how long you take to answer them.
What it catchesDoes it catch what matters?Which check fails at each gate, how many changes get through the first time and what slips through.
CostDoes it pay off?Seconds of each check, and time and tokens of each role.
ScaleDoes it hold as you grow?Minutes of work per color, with one row per change.

Each number is read with the others. If your load goes down but more slips through, you haven't saved anything: you've moved it.

.gdd/gdd metrics --all --summary
.gdd/gdd metrics --all --format csv --out <folder>

What to compare against

  • Against your own project, before and after. The baseline is measured before you start and written down in the blueprint.
  • With the DORA metrics as a reference, split by risk and size.
  • The threshold for simplifying is set in advance. If GDD doesn't justify its cost, whatever is left over gets cut.

Why not trust the feeling

In a METR trial, sixteen experienced developers took 19% longer with AI and believed they had been 20% faster.

It measures the blueprint, not the people. And no number is a target: when it becomes one, it stops measuring.

The data stays in your project. Nothing leaves on its own.
