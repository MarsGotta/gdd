Good practices

Forty-two practices, ordered by moment. They're a proposal, not a rulebook: pick the ones that work for you.

Harden, stop 3 of 3

Prepare

  1. Mark as delicate whatever is expensive if it fails.
  2. Start with few gates, but strict ones.
  3. Write each gate as a scenario.
  4. Ask for more checks the higher the risk.
  5. Confine the agent before opening its first gate.
  6. Hold back the bloat too.
  7. Adopt GDD by zones.
  8. Keep the constitution and the cross-cutting context simple and clear.
  9. Say who it is for and what would prove it doesn't help.
  10. Separate what you have seen from what you assume.
  11. Ask about what is delicate before the first line.
  12. Make the first change cross the product end to end.

Brief

  1. Give the agent short instructions, with its gates.
  2. Smaller is better, and the parts are your call.
  3. Test the checks before you trust them.
  4. For delicate changes, keep examples the agent can't see.
  5. Always ask what must not be possible.
  6. Write each rule in one line, with its figure.
  7. Say what you assume, and ask openly what you can't assume.

Execute

  1. Run the cheap stuff while working.
  2. The reviewer tests with a clean context.
  3. Give the reviewer the failures that keep repeating.
  4. Set a limit on rounds, and after that only what's new.
  5. An unstable gate doesn't burn attempts.
  6. Have the builder say what it decided.

When a person approves

  1. What arrives for approval answers four questions.
  2. An agent never approves in place of a person.
  3. Understand the part you're about to touch, not every line.
  4. Define the emergency gate before you need it.

Close

  1. Say beforehand what signal will tell you whether it helped.
  2. What you look at can't be something the builder can fake.
  3. Keep what is waiting for a verdict in plain sight.
  4. Decide in the blueprint what closes by itself.

Harden

  1. Ship with a way back and watch what happens afterward.
  2. Write down everything that slips through and which layer should have caught it.
  3. What slips through is the reviewer's exam.
  4. Measure the reviewer's noise.
  5. Now and then, review what you already accepted.
  6. Review the gates too, and retire the ones you don't need.
  7. Let the engine harden on its own, with approved thresholds. It's an idea to test: the core doesn't allow it today.
  8. Look at the report on your blueprint every so often.
  9. Compare against a baseline, not your impression.
