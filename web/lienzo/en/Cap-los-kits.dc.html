The kits

Ready-made rules by area, so you don't start from scratch. None has been published yet.

T-minus, in development, stop 2 of 2

What a kit is

A kit is ready-made rules for an area, which you start from and then adjust. It's part of the engine, but it isn't the engine: the engine is the same for every area, and what changes is the kit it loads.

Until they arrive, gdd-prepare looks up what a project like yours has to protect and proposes zones and gates, each with its source.

What it brings

  • Configuration and standing checks.
  • The tools you need to download.
  • Instructions for the agents: skills and good practices.
  • How the method is configured: for example, how much is demanded at each color.

The first kit will be the software one. Your blueprint is that kit plus your decisions.

When a check is missing

If your area needs a check that doesn't exist, you build it outside the engine and plug it in like any other. To the engine it's a command that says whether it passes or not, just like a linter. In design, for example, a gate can compare each component pixel by pixel with its reference from the design system.
