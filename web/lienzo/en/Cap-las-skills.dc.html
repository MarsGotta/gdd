The skills

Day to day: asking for a change, approving what has to pass, closing it and hardening.

Brief, stop 1 of 2

The eight skills

SkillWhat it does
gddThe orchestrator. It loads by itself when you ask for a change.
gdd-preparePrepares the project's blueprint and constitution.
gdd-briefDecides the color and, on yellow and red, writes rules and scenarios.
gdd-checksAnother agent, never the one that builds, sets up the standing checks when preparing and writes the cases of each change.
gdd-buildThe agent that builds the change and brings the facts that prove it.
gdd-reviewA reviewer with a clean context. It answers pass, fix or stop.
gdd-closeLooks at whether a change in use helped.
gdd-hardenProposes adding a check when something slips through, or relaxing or retiring one that no longer adds anything. You approve it, or your team does.

Asking for a change

You ask for it in your own words. If your blueprint asks for your approval, as the template does on yellow and red, you approve a short message with the rules, what the agent assumed and what is left as a question. Then three agents work separately: one writes the cases, another builds and another reviews and works out the color again.

The layer

The gdd command keeps the order and the log of every change. It needs Rust 1.89 or later and is built into .gdd/gdd.

.gdd/gdd next
.gdd/gdd waiting
.gdd/gdd metrics --all --summary

Close

With the change in use, you, or whoever your blueprint names, answer whether it helped: yes, not yet or no.

What is a rule today

Without an engine, the agent not touching what judges it is a rule, not a guarantee. T-minus is still in development.
