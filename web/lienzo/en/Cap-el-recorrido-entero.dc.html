The whole path

The full flow I use in my projects, with five gates counting the preparation's, and what happens when something fails.

Execute, stop 1 of 3

On every change

  1. You define what has to pass with the orchestrator. Rules and scenarios in Gherkin, which you approve or reject. Scenarios gate: person.
  2. Another agent writes the tests, e2e ones too. CI checks that they fail for the right reason and a reviewer checks that they cover the scenarios.
  3. A different agent writes the code that makes them pass, with linters, until a reviewer with a clean context passes it. Loop gate: machines.
  4. If the agent thinks a test is wrong, it stops and says so. It doesn't modify it.
  5. The loop only repeats twice. On the third time, the orchestrator decides whether the scope or the approach changes, and you talk it over.
  6. The change reaches the pull request, where the e2e tests and another reviewer run. PR gate: machines.
  7. If it touches red zones, you review the code and how it would be undone.
  8. You give the final approval. Release gate: person. GDD doesn't require it: it's a person's check I've chosen in my projects.
  9. You look at whether it helped: yes, not yet or no.
  10. You harden what failed. The orchestrator proposes rules with their test, or relaxing or retiring what no longer adds anything.

When the path goes sideways

If this happensWhat happens
The change is the text on a buttonIt goes through the same gates, with standing checks and no review from you.
There is an emergency in productionThe first thing is to try to undo it. If a fix is needed, a person approves it through the emergency gate.
Almost everything comes out redThe blueprint marks too much as delicate. .gdd/gdd metrics shows how many changes come out of each color.
You ask for a tweak when approvingIt goes back to the agent, and the gates and your approval are requested again.
Nobody looks at whether it helpedIf it promised something, it stays in use and not closed, in plain sight, without holding up what comes next. What promised nothing closes by itself after the days your blueprint sets.
A rule can't be decidedIt gets tagged @question until a later change.
