The four moments

You do Prepare once per project, and again each time you enter a new zone. Brief and Execute go with every change. And Harden only comes when something slips through or repeats.

MomentWhenWhat happens
1. PrepareOnce per project, and before the first change in each zoneYou decide what it's about, what is delicate, which gates are needed, the constitution and what context each gate carries, and you can start from ready-made rules. Machines build the gates from the blueprint, and then you try to beat them on purpose. Until they hold, no change starts in that zone.
2. BriefOn every change, before it startsYou fix what has to pass and a first estimate of the risk is made, with your approval if needed.
3. ExecuteOn every changeThe agent works and gets through the gates, one by one, and if it gets stuck, it stops and asks. At the end the risk is recalculated from what was touched, and if it goes up, the change goes back to Brief and has to get through the gates of the new level.
4. HardenWhen something slips through or repeatsWhat slipped through becomes one more check, and the reviewer already carries it in its exam.

Prepare and Brief look alike, but one builds what applies to the whole project, like the standing checks, and the other what applies only to this change, its custom check.

They aren't four meetings or four documents. In the shop, Prepare was deciding the constitution, the gates, their context and the traffic light; Brief, the scenarios and color of each change; Execute, the agent against the gates; and Harden, what we changed when someone saw another person's order.

A change, in six steps

Brief and Execute split into five steps, and Harden adds the sixth. They are the steps of the delicate case, where you see them with an example.

StepMomentWhat happens
1. You decide what has to passBriefA few scenarios and the risk of the change are written down before anyone builds.
2. An agent builds the checksBriefAn agent that won't do the work makes them, and you see them fail for the right reason.
3. Another agent gets through the gateExecuteIt works without touching the checks, and if it gets stuck, it stops and asks.
4. The gate closesExecuteThe checks run out of its reach. If they don't all pass, it doesn't go on.
5. You look only where it mattersExecuteA person approves where the blueprint asks for it, with the risk already recalculated.
6. If something slips through, you hardenHardenWhat slipped through becomes one more check.

How much each step weighs is up to your blueprint: there are changes where the first one is just your request and the fifth doesn't exist. Start today sets up the fourth, which is the first gate. The shop spreads them over the four gates that project needed, and The whole path takes them my way, with five.

Where the blueprint comes from

The first thing that comes out of Prepare is the blueprint: what is delicate, which gates are needed and what each level asks for. You don't have to start from scratch, because you can start from ready-made rules for your area, which is the idea behind The kits.

The blueprint doesn't ask anything new of you: it's put together with what you already have in the repository. How, in Start today.
