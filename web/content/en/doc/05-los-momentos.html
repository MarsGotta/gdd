The four moments

You do Prepare once per project, and again each time you enter a new area. Brief and Execute go with every change. And Harden only comes when something slips through or repeats.

MomentWhenWhat happens
1. PrepareOnce per project, and before the first change in each areaYou decide what it's about, what is delicate, which gates are needed, the constitution and what context each gate carries, and you can start from ready-made rules. Machines build the gates from the blueprint, and then you try to beat them on purpose. Until they hold, no change starts in that area.
2. BriefOn every change, before it startsYou fix what has to pass and a first estimate of the risk is made, with your approval if needed.
3. ExecuteOn every changeThe agent works and gets through the gates, one by one, and if it gets stuck, it stops and asks. At the end the risk is recalculated from what was touched, and if it goes up, the change has to get through the gates of the new level.
4. HardenWhen something slips through or repeatsWhat slipped through becomes one more check, and the reviewer already carries it in its exam.

Prepare and Brief look alike, but one builds what applies to the whole project, like the standard checks, and the other what applies only to this change, its custom check.

They aren't four meetings or four documents. In the shop, Prepare was deciding the constitution, the gates, their context and the traffic light; Brief, the scenarios and color of each change; Execute, the agent against the gates; and Harden, what we changed when someone saw another person's order.

Where the blueprint comes from

The first thing that comes out of Prepare is the blueprint: what is delicate, which gates are needed and what each level asks for. You don't have to start from scratch, because you can start from ready-made rules for your area, which is the idea behind The kits.

The blueprint doesn't ask anything new of you: it's put together with what you already have in the repository. How, in Start today.
