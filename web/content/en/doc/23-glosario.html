Glossary

Most carry in parentheses the name the Spanish edition of these pages gives them.

The method

Gate (puerta)
What a change has to get through to go in. It's passed only if all its checks pass, whatever the agent says. The idea.
Check (comprobación)
How a gate is validated. There are three types: machine, such as a test or a linter; reviewer, another agent that reviews; and person, your approval. The pieces and the roles.
Standing checks (comprobaciones de serie)
The ones set up once for the whole project that every change goes through, such as the seal, the tests or lint. They're tried on purpose before any change starts.
The five moments
Prepare (Preparar), at the start and whenever the blueprint falls short; Brief (Encargar) and Execute (Ejecutar), on every change; Close (Cerrar), once it's in use; and Harden (Blindar), when something slips through or a gate is too much. The five moments.
Core (núcleo)
What isn't negotiable in GDD, and what you can demand of any engine that claims to apply it. The core.
Context
What you give the agent so it can get through a gate. It helps, and it proves nothing. A clean context is what an agent gets without the others' conversation.

The project

Blueprint (plano)
Your project's rules: its gates, its zones, what each color asks for, which model does each job and its limits. You approve it, or your team does. What it leaves in your repository.
Constitution (constitución)
A short document with what must never happen in your product, its stack and its commands. Every agent carries it, at every gate.
Zone (zona)
A set of paths in your project with a color, by how much damage a change there can do. What no zone covers is never green, and it isn't red by default either: its color is worked out on each change.
Color
Green, yellow or red: the risk of a change, which comes from the most delicate thing it touches. Your blueprint says what a change of each color has to prove and who approves it. What each color asks for.
Limits (límites)
What a change of each color can spend: rounds, minutes and cost. When one runs out, no new round starts and your agent proposes a way out.
Level (nivel)
Light, standard or deep: how much the model doing a job reasons. The blueprint says which model each level is in each agent you use. What each color asks for.
Debt (deuda)
A check that already fails without the change, recorded with its baseline so it doesn't hold everything up. Recording it loosens the check, so you decide it.

A change

Brief (encargo)
What has to pass for a change, written before anyone builds: the rules, their scenarios, how you'll know it helped and how to undo it. The skills.
Rule (regla)
What has to be true in your product, in one sentence. It goes above the scenarios that check it. @assumed marks a rule the agent chose; @question, one that can wait for another change.
Scenario (escenario)
An example of a rule, in Gherkin, with a single action and concrete values. Each one carries its @id and ends up as a test.
Seal (sello)
The brief, its scenarios and its tests, kept on the gdd-seal/<id> branch before anyone builds. The seal check makes sure nobody touched what judges the change.
Cases
The tests of a change, one per scenario, which another agent writes and sees failing before anyone builds.
Round
One pass of building and going through the gates. If a check fails, the change goes back with what failed, in another round, while there are rounds left.
In use (en uso)
A change that's already on your main branch and that nobody has closed yet. It waits in sight, without holding up the next ones.
Verdict
At Close, your answer to whether the change helped: yes, not yet or no. The skills.
What slips through
A failure that got past every gate. Harden looks for the crack, what the gate was missing to catch it, and keeps it as a check.

Who does what

Role (papel)
Each job in a change that a separate agent does: writing the cases, building and reviewing. Each one starts without the others' conversation.
Orchestrator
The agent that hands out the work, with the gdd skill. It prepares, briefs, closes and hardens with you, and builds nothing. The pieces and the roles.
Agent, or the builder (el que construye)
The one that makes the change and gets through the gates, on a branch of its own, without seeing the log or how its cases were made.
Reviewer (vigilante)
An agent with a clean context that reviews the change: pass, fix or stop. On yellow it reads the change against its brief, and on red it also runs it and says what it ran. Each finding of a fix carries its citation and, on red, the command that reproduces it. Where you approve, it leaves you evidence. On red, and on yellow when the change alters another zone's data, it also reads the brief before anyone builds.
Skill
A file of instructions your agent loads when it needs it. GDD comes with eight: an entry skill and seven more, one per moment or role. The skills.

The guarantees

Layer (capa)
The gdd command that comes with the skills: it keeps the order of every change, counts its limits and writes its log. It isn't an engine: an agent runs it, so it's within the agent's reach, and what keeps it honest is your CI. The layer's commands.
Log (registro)
Everything that happened in a change, one event per line, with who approved what. The layer writes it and your CI checks it's whole.
Degrees
How far what judges the agent is out of its reach: rule (norma), if it's only asked to; platform (plataforma), if your protected branches and permissions stop it; engine (motor), if an engine stops it. How a gate gets fooled.
Engine (motor)
What enforces the whole core without depending on the agent obeying. Any engine that meets it will do, including one you write yourself. T-minus is the one I'm building, and it's still in development. The engine.
Hidden examples
More examples of the same scenarios, kept where the builder can't read them, to catch a solution tailored to the tests.
Ready-made rules
The gates and standing checks of an area, ready to start from. None has been published yet: they'll come with T-minus. The kits.

What changed in each version of the skills, and what you do when updating, is in What's new.
