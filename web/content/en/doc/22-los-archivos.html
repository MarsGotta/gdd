What it leaves in your repository

Everything GDD does ends up in files you can read, which make sense without the skills. An engine will read and write the same ones, under the same contract, {{contrato}}, so what you set up today will still serve you later. Every file, with its template, is in the contract.

What Prepare leaves

FileWhat it holdsWho writes itAnd you
gdd/blueprint.mdThe blueprint: gates, zones, colors, checks, models, limits and who closes.Your agent, when preparing and hardening.You approve it. Any change to it goes through you, or your team.
gdd/gates/One feature per gate: what it asks of each color and what it rejects.Same as the blueprint.Same as the blueprint.
gdd/constitution.mdFive lines about your product and, after them, the stack, the commands and the rules that hold everywhere.Your agent when preparing, in your words.You approve it, and every brief is checked against it.
gdd/check.sh and gdd/limits.shHow long each check takes, and what a change has left of its limits.They come with the skills, if you take them when preparing.They're yours from then on.
gdd/debt.mdKnown debt, with the baseline of each check.Your agent, never on its own.Recording debt loosens a check: you decide it.
gdd/context/Copies of the sources a gate needs.Your agent when preparing.You can read them to see where a proposal came from.
AGENTS.mdA GDD block, under 100 words, with the core and the map.Your agent when preparing.The rest of the file is still yours. If you have a CLAUDE.md, it adds a line @AGENTS.md to it, because Claude Code reads AGENTS.md only when there's none.
CODEOWNERSWhat you, or your team, always approve: the blueprint, the gates and the red zones, among others.Your agent when preparing.It's yours. Your platform enforces it once you apply the protection of the main branch your agent gives you.
gdd/log/blueprint.jsonlThe log of what isn't about one change: approving the blueprint, its gates or its debt, preparing and hardening. Setting up the standing checks goes in gdd/log/standing-checks.jsonl.The layer.Never by hand.
.gdd/gddThe built layer. Git ignores it: every copy of the project builds its own.Your agent, the first time and whenever the skills are newer.Nothing.

What every change leaves

FileWhat it holdsWho writes itAnd you
gdd/briefs/<id>.mdThe brief of a yellow or red change: what you asked for, what has to pass, how it closes and how to undo it.The orchestrator.You approve it, or tell it what to change.
features/<capability>.featureThe rules and their scenarios, with @assumed and @question where they apply.The orchestrator.You approve it with the brief.
gdd/log/<id>.jsonlThe log: everything that happened in the change, one event per line, with who approved what.The layer.Never by hand: a mistake gets corrected with a new event.
gdd/log/<id>.engine.jsonlThe layer's journal: each time it was called, and what it refused.The layer.Nothing.
gdd/backlog.mdWhat's left to build, each item with its expected color, and at most one hardening proposal per change.Your agents, on every change.It doesn't ask for your review. You read it when you want to know what's coming.
gdd/exam.mdThe changes that got past a reviewer, to examine the next ones with.Your agent, when hardening.You approve it with the hardening.
The gdd-seal/<id> branchThe seal: the brief, its scenarios and its tests, as they were approved.The orchestrator and the agent that writes the cases.Nobody merges it. On red, you can approve the brief in a pull request from it.

Updating the skills touches none of these files: the plugin lives in your agent and your files in your project. The only thing that gets rebuilt is the layer, and your agent does it (What's new).
