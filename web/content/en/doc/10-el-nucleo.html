The core

You can adapt almost everything in this documentation: how many gates you put up, what you ask for at each one, which practices work for you. What comes next you can't.

It's what you can demand of any engine that says it applies GDD, mine included, and no configuration can remove it. There are four guarantees.

The agent doesn't touch the blueprint or the checks

  • Whoever goes through a gate doesn't decide how it's validated, and doesn't act as the reviewer of their own work.
  • There are no exceptions by rank: no agent, not even the orchestrator, can modify a check once it's sealed (that is, out of its reach), change the rules that decide the risk or skip a gate.
  • If a test is wrong, fixing it creates another version. If the fix changes what has to pass and the blueprint asks for that approval, it goes back to a person.
  • The order of the gates doesn't depend on a model's goodwill: code enforces it.

The risk is calculated from what was touched, not from what was declared. If the agent says it changed some text and it turns out it touched payment, it's red.

That's why it's recalculated at the end, and it can only go up: if it does, the change goes back to Brief and has to pass the checks of the new level.

The possible damage is bounded

  • The agent works without access to production and with just the credentials it needs.
  • A person confirms anything that can't be undone.
  • In red, besides, nothing ships without a verified way to undo it. If there isn't one, a person decides knowing that.

Some decisions are made only by a person

  • If the blueprint changes, you approve it, or your team does. This can't be turned off, and accepting ready-made rules also counts as changing the blueprint.
  • The same goes for loosening: taking a check off a gate, or retiring it, is also changing the blueprint, and it never happens without approval.
  • A person says whether a change in use has helped: you, or whoever answers for the product. Telling the agent to decide doesn't hand that decision over.
  • An approval only counts if it arrives by a path no agent has access to. An agent's approval never takes the place of a person's, and yours is valid only for what you saw.

What has to pass is approved by the author at the levels where the blueprint asks for it. And having a person review what's red? I recommend it, but it isn't required.

Each project configures both, and no longer asking for an approval the blueprint used to ask for is also loosening.

Emergencies get thought through before they happen, too. They have their own gate, defined in advance: a person opens it, it's recorded, and the change then goes through the normal gates.

Nothing is left going in circles, and nothing happens unseen

  • The attempts, time and cost of each change have a limit, and when it runs out, the engine stops it and asks you.
  • Everything is recorded: who did what, which gate judged it and how much it cost.

The skills and their layer already cover almost all of the above, though with weaker guarantees. An agent runs the layer, so it's within the agent's reach like any file in the repository, and what keeps it honest is your CI.
