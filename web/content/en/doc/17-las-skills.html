The skills

You've set up your first gate by hand and it works. The next change you'd like to leave entirely to your agents: one writes the scenarios with you, another builds the checks, a third does the work, and someone who has seen none of that reviews it. That's a lot to explain every time, so it's written down once, in seven skills.

A skill is a file of instructions your agent loads when it needs it. The GDD ones install nothing and carry no code: they use what your project already has, its tests, its linter, its CI and git. One is the entry skill, gdd, which hands out the work, and there are six more, one per role.

The seven

SkillWho loads itWhat it leaves
gddThe orchestrator. It loads by itself when you ask for a changeWhat comes next, and who gets launched.
gdd-prepareThe orchestrator, with youThe blueprint, the constitution and the gates of each zone.
gdd-briefThe orchestrator, on every changeWhat has to pass, as scenarios, and the expected risk.
gdd-checksAnother agent, never the one that buildsThe checks, each seen failing for the right reason.
gdd-buildThe agent that goes through the gateThe change, and the facts that prove it.
gdd-reviewThe reviewer, with a clean contextIts verdict, and the risk worked out again.
gdd-hardenThe orchestrator, with you, when something slips throughThe crack, and the check that would have caught it.

Whoever builds the checks, whoever builds the change and whoever reviews it each work in a separate agent, which starts knowing nothing of what the others did. That way the reviewer judges the change in front of it, without having heard how it was made (The pieces and the roles).

How to install them

Copy the seven folders from the repository into your agent's skills folder: its README says which one that is for each agent. Then ask your agent to set GDD up, or ask it for a change. The first thing it will do is sit down with you to decide what is delicate in your project and which gates it needs.

That same repository holds the tests the skills are checked with, run with a real model.

What they leave in your repository

Everything ends up in files you can read, in a gdd/ folder: the blueprint, the gates of each zone written as scenarios, the brief of each change and a log of what happened, with who approved what. Those files make sense without the skills. An engine will read and write the same ones, so what you set up today will still serve you later.

What they don't guarantee

What they don't give you is a guarantee. Without an engine, the agent not touching what judges it is a rule: the skills tell it so and then check that it hasn't, but nothing stops it. With protected branches and the permissions of your platform you get a good part of the way, and your blueprint says which of the three degrees you're at (How a gate gets fooled). Only an engine gives the full guarantee, and what's missing without one is in The engine.

With skills or by hand, what is delicate stays delicate. What changes when the thing you touch is payment, I tell in A delicate case.
