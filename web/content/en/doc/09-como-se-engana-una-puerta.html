How a gate gets fooled

A gate can be fooled without touching the test, and there are four ways to do it.

Four tricks

TrickExampleHow it's stopped
LyingSays "everything passes" without running itSomeone else runs it
Faking the resultPrints 3 passed, or leaves a fake vitest in node_modulesSomeone else runs it, on a clean copy
Changing what surrounds the test"test": "exit 0" in package.json, or a hook in conftest.py that skips every testCompare those files with what was approved. CI alone is not enough, because it runs whatever is on the branch
Cheating from the codeif (name === "Ana") return "Hello, Ana", or if (process.env.VITEST)No tool prevents it: more cases, hidden examples or a reviewer that runs it

That hook in conftest.py has already happened: OpenAI saw a model add it.

ImpossibleBench classifies four cheating strategies, and three of them don't modify the test.

To practise, the workshop puts six gates in front of you and asks whether they hold.

Rule, platform and engine

The fifth principle starts out as a rule, and a rule works without an engine: you ask the agent and review the diff. That works as long as you keep looking.

The guarantee comes when the agent can't touch the check even if it tries. Your platform's protected branches and permissions get you a good part of the way:

  • By hand: you set them up yourself, in A gate by hand.
  • With the skills: your agent writes the CODEOWNERS, you apply the rest and it tries them.

Only an engine gives the full guarantee, where not even whoever administers the repository can loosen it without a person's approval. That makes three degrees, and each one depends less on the agent obeying. The core is the list of those guarantees.

DegreeWhat holds the agent backWhat you get it withHow far it goes
RuleIts word: you ask itA line in its instructions or some skills, and you looking at the diff. With the skills, the seal check finds out afterwards, but doesn't prevent itIt works as long as you keep looking. Nothing stops it
PlatformYour platform doesn't let itProtected branches, permissions, CI and an agent with its own credentialsA good part of the way. Whoever administers can still loosen it
EngineCode out of its reach that keeps count of the whole changeAn engine that meets the coreThe full guarantee

Your blueprint says which of the three you're at.

The skills' layer also keeps count of the whole change, but while an agent runs it, it's within that agent's reach: with it you're still on rule or platform.

Is CI mandatory?

No. What's mandatory is that the agent doesn't judge itself. The test can be run by CI, by an engine or by you on a clean copy.

CI is just the cheapest way not to depend on your discipline. With the skills, it's also where the layer goes over each change's log and runs its gates out of the agents' reach. Without it, that's on you.

What really stops these tricks is the guarantees, so the question is which ones you can demand. The whole list is in The core.

CI passes on the agent's branch. Is that enough to know nothing around the test has changed?

  • Yes: if CI runs it, the agent couldn't have faked the result.
  • No: CI runs whatever is on the branch, a changed package.json or conftest.py included.

That's it. To find out, you compare those files with what was approved.

It isn't enough. CI doesn't fake anything, but it runs what the agent left on its branch: you have to compare those files with what was approved.
