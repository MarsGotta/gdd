The kits

Imagine you're starting a project from scratch and you have to decide every rule yourself: what gets checked, with which tools, what instructions each agent receives.

That's what kits are for: ready-made rules for an area, which you start from and then adjust. They are part of the engine, but they are not the engine: the engine is the same for every area, and what changes is the kit it loads.

No kit has been published yet: they'll come with T-minus. Meanwhile, when you set up your project, gdd-prepare looks up what a project like yours has to protect and proposes zones and gates, each with its source (The skills).

What a kit brings

  • Its configuration and its standing checks.
  • The tools you need to download.
  • The instructions for the agents, like skills and good practices.
  • How the method is configured, for example how much is demanded at each color of the risk table.

The one T-minus will bring is the software kit. Your blueprint is that kit plus your decisions, or your team's, and each change then adds the cases of its custom checks.

Choosing a kit changes your blueprint, and you decide that, or your team does (The core).

If you look inside the engine, you'll find three more words:

Seal
To put a check out of the agent's reach.
Box
The bounded environment where the agent works.
Log
What keeps who did what, which gate judged it and what it cost.

When a check is missing

If your area needs a check that doesn't exist, you build it outside the engine and plug it in like any other. To the engine it's the same as Cucumber or a linter: a command that says whether it passes or not.

In design, for example, a gate can compare each component, pixel by pixel, with its reference from the design system and its tokens.

And like any check, the agent can't touch it, neither the script nor the references.

With a kit you'll have somewhere to start. The rest of the documentation is there to look up when you need it, starting with what to do when a change stops, in When it stops.

Does each area need its own engine?

  • No: the engine is the same for all of them, and what changes is the kit it loads.
  • Yes: each area brings its own engine, with its rules inside.
