Workshop: does this gate hold?

Before you take a gate as good, the Manifesto asks for four answers, one for each of principles 1, 4, 5 and 2 in The seven principles. If one is no, the gate doesn't hold yet. Four yeses aren't enough either: it also has to check what matters, and your blueprint decides that.

Here you take one away at a time and see where the error slips through. Then you decide whether six gates hold.

Build the gate

The change is the discount codes of A delicate case. Before starting, two scenarios were written: with the 10% code, a €50 cart comes to €45, and an €80 one to €72. The agent brings a solution tailored to the first one, which gives €70 for €80, and says "done."

  • Was it decided before the agent started? If not: the check was written after seeing its work, so it checks what it did and not what was needed, and the €80 cart wasn't there.
  • Is it checked without trusting what the agent says? If not: the agent said its tests passed and nobody ran them. Saying it opened the gate.
  • Is it out of the agent's reach, so that touching it does the agent no good? If not: the agent changed what surrounds the test, for example "test": "exit 0" in package.json, and CI ran what was on its branch.
  • Have you seen it reject something done wrong, real or planted on purpose? If not: nobody ever saw it fail, and it lets the error through just as it would have let anything through.

With all four answers on yes, the gate says no: the €80 cart fails, the error goes back to the agent and you don't even notice.

With any of them on no, the gate opens and the error reaches production.
